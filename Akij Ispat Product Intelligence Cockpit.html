What this answers

How strongly volume responds to price, SKU by SKU.

What β means

β is own-price elasticity — the % change in volume for a 1% change in price. β = −2: a 10% price rise is linked to a ~20% volume drop. β = −0.5: a 10% rise only trims volume ~5%. β > 0: price and volume move together (scrap-cost pass-through, not demand).

How to read it

Hover any bar, point or table cell for the full interpretation. Volume is derived (revenue ÷ price), so β is directional, not a causal demand curve. R² shows how reliable the fit is; Pairs is the number of month-over-month observations used.

Elasticity by SKU (β = Δln volume ÷ Δln price)

First-difference estimator over consecutive clean months. Teal = β<0 (normal: price up → volume down). Red = β>0 (anomalous).

Price vs volume, within SKU

Each point = one SKU-month; ln(price) and ln(volume) demeaned to the SKU's own mean. Colour = sign of that SKU's β.

Elasticity table

Read with care. Volume is derived (revenue ÷ price), so β is directional only — demand here is project-driven and stock-outs, discounts and freight distort the estimate. Treat β as a signal to investigate, not a causal demand curve.

Method

  • Net price = realised invoice value ÷ MT, after discounts.
  • GP% = (net price − avg cost) ÷ net price. Est. NP% = GP% − ~2.1% (commission ~1.3% + freight ~0.8%).
  • Volume (MT) in MoM & elasticity views = monthly net revenue ÷ realised net price — derived, directional only.
  • Elasticity β = OLS slope of Δln(volume) on Δln(price) between consecutive months; needs ≥ 8 pairs and |β| ≤ 8.
  • Move detection = |Δprice| ≥ 2% MoM with ratio guard 0.5–2 (excludes unit breaks).
  • Mixed-unit rebar quantity entries from 2023–24 are excluded from per-ton series (revenue values remain valid).
  • Last-month convention: the running month (Nov'26) is partial and excluded from "current" statistics.
